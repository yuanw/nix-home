** Phase 3 is run: blocking is live, and the lists were in the wrong place

2026-10-17, gen 22 → 23 → 24 → 25 → 26. Gen 23 declared both lists *under*
=settings.dns=, loaded one of them, and refused to budge for four activations after
that. The cause was never AdAway and never the version:

*In schema 32 the filter list is a top-level key.* =/var/lib/AdGuardHome/AdGuardHome.yaml=
puts =filters:= next to =whitelist_filters:= and =user_rules:=, all three of them
outside =dns:=, which is where 0.107.71 reads them. A list declared inside
=settings.dns.filters= is parsed and dropped, and AGH then falls back to the
filter pair it has compiled in — AdGuard DNS filter (enabled) + AdAway Default
Blocklist (disabled). That is the whole of the "AdAway will not load" and
"removing a filter declaratively does not remove it" behaviour, and it is worth
noting that =yaml-merge= was innocent both times: its list rule is =return b=, a
plain replacement, which the tool's 30 lines of Python say in one line. The *2.6*
experiment that "proved" the merge was decisive was a test of the merge, not of
AGH.

*This was our bug, not the ecosystem's.* The same code search that prompted this
section found 15 configs with a =filters= key and all 15 declare it as a sibling of
=dns=, where AGH reads it. One of them is worth reading as a reference for shape,
not for content: =mx42/homelab='s =config/dns-adguardhome.nix= puts =filters= first,
then =dns=, exactly as the yaml ends up. So the correct answer to "do people
declare more than one list?" is yes, and they declare them in the place that
works; the failure here was that we wrote ours one nesting level too deep and got
no signal for it.

Three things this file said that the run corrected, restated where they belong:

- =blocking_mode= is undeclared and its =default= answers a blocked name with

- AdAway's file is *hosts* format (`127.0.0.1 host`), and its own header says

  in *3.6* actually use.
- The house was already filtered server-side by AdGuard's resolver before any of

** The premise of decision 7 does not hold: the e8450 never filtered

*AdGuard Home is installed on the e8450 and is not in any DNS path* (confirmed
2026-10-17; worth a re-check if this doc outlives the AP). The cutover's
"What exists today" table says dnsmasq forwards to AdGuardHome — it does not. The
live dhcp config is ~server = 127.0.0.1#5053 / 127.0.0.1#5054~, which is
https-dns-proxy doing Cloudflare DoH, and =openwrt/routers/e8450.nix= declares
only the UCI stub (workdir + config_file), no filters and no upstream. Nothing
routes to an AGH listen port.

That changes the shape of this work:

- *Decision 7 is net-new, not a parity requirement.* No house behaviour depends
  on AGH today, so nothing here is at risk of being lost in the migration, and
  there is no "the swap broke adblocking" incident to recover from. Declare
  =filters = [ ]= and the change is a no-op for every client.
- *Nothing to migrate.* =/etc/adguardhome/adguardhome.yaml= on the AP is the
  default/no-rule config, so there is no state to copy and no rules to diff
  against. The lists are a fresh choice.
- *The parity item is privacy, not blocking, and the board is behind.* The AP
  resolved over Cloudflare DoH and =force_dns=1= redirected everything into it;
  the board forwards plaintext UDP to whatever the ISP hands out in
  ~/etc/resolv.conf~. That is the property actually lost at the swap — which is
  why decision 1 below is the more important half of this plan, not the filters.

* Design

** The hop order

Clients → =AGH 192.168.1.1:53= → =dnsmasq 127.0.0.1:5354= → =94.140.14.14=/=1.1.1.1=

AGH took the front door on 2026-10-18 (gen 31) and dnsmasq became what it asks.
Neither service changed job, only the order they sit in, and clients still
configure nothing: option 6 in DHCP is still 192.168.1.1, and the port each one
listens on is the whole of the edit. dnsmasq still answers everything it owns —
leases and their =.lan= names (=dgx-spark.lan=, needed by misfit's caddy vhosts),
the static leases, ~address = /.yuanw.me/192.168.1.190~, =local=/lan/=,
=bogus-priv= — but it hears those queries from AGH now, not from the house.

- *Why 5354 and not loopback.* Measured with a throwaway instance before
  deploying: =bind-interfaces= + =interface = br-lan= makes dnsmasq bind
  =192.168.1.1:53= whatever =listen-address= says, so dnsmasq cannot be parked on
  loopback while keeping =:53= — it would only win the port back from AGH. Moving
  it to 5354 does the job, and the LAN cannot reach it there: =chain input= is
  =policy drop= and no rule names 5354, checked from a client with
  =dig -p 5354 example.com @192.168.1.1=, which gets nothing. So the guarantee that
  nothing gets around the filter is firewall default now, not a bind — give 5354 an
  accept rule and the bypass is back.
- *Why AGH's single upstream has to be dnsmasq.* 0.107.71 has one upstream list
  and no per-domain routing — probed the binary for domain-specific-upstream keys
  and for =local_dns_hosts=, and neither is there (both are 1.x). AGH cannot send
  =.lan= to dnsmasq and the rest to the public pair, so dnsmasq is the only
  upstream. That is what keeps =.lan=, the leases and =*.yuanw.me= answering with
  nothing rebuilt, and it is why decision 1's public pair moved down a hop.
- *What the order costs* is two services in series instead of one plus a hop. A
  dead dnsmasq is a full outage now — =.lan=, the statics and =*.yuanw.me= go with
  external resolution — so =systemd.services.dnsmasq= carries =RestartSec= and
  =StartLimitIntervalSec = 0=. A failed =address already in use= across a restart
  is otherwise the house offline, and that failure is specific to a swap where two
  units want each other's old ports.

** How AGH took =:53= (the decision this reverses)

The 2026-10-17 plan said no. Its objection stands, so it is kept below rather than
deleted — but the answer to it changed one day later, and that is the part worth
writing down.

*Why not put AGH on =:53= instead* — kept as written 2026-10-17:

The alternative is AGH owning port 53 and dnsmasq dropping to =port = 0= (DHCP
only). Rejected:

- Everything dnsmasq serves from leases/statics/=address=/ has to be rebuilt
  inside AGH (=local_dns_hosts= + rewrites), and a name built off a lease cannot
  be pinned there — that is the exact =dgx-spark.lan= problem the cutover already
  had to solve once.
- It changes two things at once on a router one day into service, for a feature
  with no measured need yet.
- It costs the loopback property, which is what makes the later VPN/unbound work
  cheap (see *Later additions*).

One line against a rewrite. Take the line.

*Superseded 2026-10-18.* The first two objections are answered by keeping dnsmasq
in the chain instead of switching it off: nothing gets rebuilt, because =.lan= and
the leases stay dnsmasq's and reach it from behind AGH. The need that made it worth
doing is measured — per-client rows in the filter's own log, which the old order
cannot produce (see *What the query log can say about a client*). The third bullet
is still true and is now the price: the loopback property is gone, so *Later
additions* has to be re-read before the VPN/unbound work starts.

Activated as gen 31 with =activation exit: 0= and no failed units. The bind check
came out =udp+tcp 192.168.1.1:53 = AdGuardHome= and =192.168.1.1:5354 = dnsmasq=,
with 23 leases still in the file and =DHCP, sockets bound exclusively to interface
br-lan= in the journal. From a house client afterwards: =example.com= resolving,
=doubleclick.net= =0.0.0.0=, =dgx-spark.lan= =.185=, =openwrt.yuanw.me= =.190=, and
=dig -p 5354 @192.168.1.1= getting nothing, which is what says the bypass stayed
shut.

** Two details that are not obvious from the module docs

- *5354 was always the port, and it still is — on the other service.* For the
  2026-10-17 shape it kept AGH from ever racing dnsmasq for =:53= across a restart,
  which is the failure mode that looks like "the house resolves nothing". After the
  swap the same port sits on dnsmasq for the same reason, so the invariant to
  preserve across any future change is simply: *the two services must never want
  =:53= at the same time.* (The other half of that invariant is ordering, which is
  why =dnsmasq= is =after = [ "adguardhome.service" ]= — if they restart out of
  order the last one to bind 5354 loses and the house has no resolver until the
  retry lands.)
- *The web UI's bind is the exposure decision.* =host = 127.0.0.1= and
  =port = 3000= are options on the service, *not* keys in =settings= — current
  nixpkgs asserts =settings.bind_host= away as "superseded by
  =services.adguardhome.host=", and since =host= defaults to =0.0.0.0=, setting it
  explicitly *is* the decision. Phase 1 confirmed the mapping: at schema 32 they
  land on =http.address:=, and no =bind_host= key exists anywhere.

  The first version kept the UI on loopback and read it over
  =ssh -N -L 3000:127.0.0.1:3000 root@192.168.1.1=: no =openFirewall =  true=
  (that is a global =tcp dport 3000=, exactly the class of unscoped rule that
  already put ssh on the WAN side), no =br-lan= rule, no =host = "192.168.1.1"=
  coupling the service to an address a future generation might move. Its cost
  turned out to be the runbooks: every check in this file is an ssh session, and
  nobody reads a log that needs a tunnel to open.

  *Decision 2 as run* settled that, and two things stayed true across the move:
  =dns.bind_hosts= is still 127.0.0.1, so moving the UI did not move the DNS
  listener, and the argument against =openFirewall =  true= is unchanged — the
  rule that came in is scoped to =br-lan=.

*** 2.6 What Phase 2 must NOT be followed by

No rule lists are declared (=filters = [ ]=), so the natural next move is to add
them in the UI. *Before doing that*, read this: =mutableSettings = true= merges
declarative config *over* the state file with =yaml-merge=, and =yaml-merge=
replaces lists rather than unioning them. Verified on dgx-spark with the
=yaml-merge= this closure uses (the store path is the same derivation the
=adguardhome= unit runs):

#+begin_src bash
Y=/nix/store/l185hixf1zn5bx04frv2kcwrfqf4c3s5-yaml-merge-0-unstable-2022-01-12/bin/yaml-merge
# state file with a UI-added list, then the declarative config
$Y <(printf 'dns:\n  filters:\n  - {enabled: true, id: 1, url: https://a.txt}\n  cache_size: 4194304\n') \
   <(printf 'dns:\n  filters: []\n')
# -> dns: {cache_size: 4194304, filters: []}        the UI's list is gone

# control: declare nothing about filters and the list survives
$Y <(printf 'dns:\n  filters:\n  - {enabled: true, id: 1, url: https://a.txt}\n  cache_size: 4194304\n') \
   <(printf 'dns:\n  cache_ttl_min: 300\n')
# -> dns: {cache_size: 4194304, cache_ttl_min: 300, filters: [{…https://a.txt}]}
#+end_src

=dnsmasq.conf= is unaffected by this, so nothing in 2.0–2.5 changes — but every
=switch-to-configuration= would silently wipe whatever the UI added. Pick one
before adding lists:

1. drop =filters = [ ]= from =hosts/mono-gateway/default.nix= and manage lists in
   the UI, where they then persist; or
2. declare the lists there, which keeps the whole filter set in a diff and is
   the only variant that survives a re-flash.

Option 2 is what this file's own conventions point to. It is a Phase 3 change,
not a Phase 2 one.

** Phase 3 in detail — rule lists, declared in nix

Everything so far is mechanism: the hop works, nothing is blocked. Phase 3 is the
one line that makes the plan's title true, and it is the shortest change in the
whole rollout — but it is the first step that can *break a house service*, so it
is staged.

Two things to start settled:

- *Phase 3 does not wait on decision 4.* The fallback only fires when AGH is
  *unreachable*: dnsmasq tries 5354, gets refused, asks the ISP. A *blocked*
  answer is an answer — NXDOMAIN comes back through dnsmasq and nothing else is
  consulted. So the lists work as well today as they would after =no-resolv =
  true=; what changes later is only what happens when AGH is down.
- *Declare them, do not click them.* =yaml-merge= replaces lists, so a list added
  in the UI is wiped by the next rebuild (*2.6*, verified). The house convention
  is that router policy is reviewable in git, and this is policy. Read that with
  the *Phase 3 as run* correction attached: the merge claim is true of the file,
  and until the declaration lands at the *top level* there is nothing for it to
  replace, which is why UI additions seemed to survive for so long.

*** 3.4 The first day, and what to do when a real service breaks

A day of real traffic before widening anything — one browser tells you nothing
about the TV, and the query log is the only evidence available.

#+begin_src bash
# from the board, newest first (the ring is ?limit=N, the search endpoint is 404)
ssh root@192.168.1.1 "curl -s 'localhost:3000/control/querylog?limit=100&response_type=json' | tail -c 800"
# or the UI over the tunnel: ssh -N -f -L 3000:127.0.0.1:3000 root@192.168.1.1
#+end_src

This is where =REJECTED= entries for something like a push domain or a login node
turn up before anyone says "the internet is slow".

When something real breaks, three levers, fastest first:

1. **Pause filtering.** The UI's protection switch is the quiet way; it costs the
   same thing as stopping the unit now, so prefer:
   #+begin_src bash
   ssh root@192.168.1.1 systemctl stop adguardhome
   #+end_src
   Since *decision 4* this is not "back to unfiltered DNS" — with no fallback
   route, external names fail while =.lan= names keep working (*2.4*). It is still
   the right lever when a rule is misfiring, because a blocked answer is an answer
   and a stopped AGH is a *missing* one, but it is a bigger deal than it was, so
   for a single domain prefer the allowlist (2) or (3).
2. **Allowlist the domain properly.** This is now verified: the UI's rule box writes
   the *top-level* =user_rules:= key in =/var/lib/AdGuardHome/AdGuardHome.yaml=
   (line 109 today), not a file under =data/filters/=. That page said "unverified"
   because the earlier guess was wrong -- =data/= contains nothing but the two rule
   files, =querylog.json=, =sessions.db= and =stats.db=. So the persistence question
   has a clean answer: **a UI-added rule survives a rebuild**, because =settings=
   declares neither =user_rules= nor =whitelist_filters= and the merge only touches
   keys the declaration declares. Declaring them is a different story:

   #+begin_src nix
     # top level, siblings of filters -- inside settings.dns they do nothing,
     # exactly as filters do there. A declared list REPLACES whatever the UI
     # added, so declare it before anyone edits the box, not after.
     user_rules = [ "@@||example.org^" ];      # the UI's own rule syntax
     whitelist_filters = [ ];                   # allowlist *lists*, not domains
   #+end_src

   Which is the whole trade: undeclared, the box is convenient and the rule lives
   outside =git diff= forever. Declared, it is reviewed like any other router
   policy and wins every rebuild. Per the house convention, policy goes in git --
   so if you find yourself adding a permanent rule, move it here, and check
   =grep -n '^user_rules:' /var/lib/AdGuardHome/AdGuardHome.yaml= afterwards to be
   sure the two did not diverge.
3. **Drop the list.** Revert one entry in =filters= and redeploy. With two lists
   this is a real option, which is another reason 3.1 starts at two.

After the day: clean log, both tables pass → widen. There is one follow-up already
booked for that, and it is a swap rather than an addition: *3.7*. Anything real
blocked → fix per 3.4 and do not widen until it has been quiet. There is no goal
that says how many lists the house needs; the mechanism is already live, and "two

*** 3.6 What everyone else declares, and which of it we should copy

Prompted by a GitHub code search for adguard+filters in Nix — 30 configs, 10 read

*They all declare it as a sibling of =dns=,* which is exactly where *Phase 3 as run*

=settings.filters=, 0 nested. The lesson is the other way round from what this

evidence at all about whether a given box is *loading* it — =rules_count= is the
only thing that answers that, on any config, ours or theirs.

| HaGeZi Pro / Pro+ / Ultimate | ~80k / ~150k / ~330k | the community default: current, ABP-format, tiers you can pick by how aggressive you want |
| OISD Big (=https://big.oisd.nl=) | ~100k+ | one URL, broad, famous for low false positives |
| 1Hosts Lite / mini, Dan Pollock, the malware/phishing registry lists | small | cheap second entries, several of the sampled configs use one as a privacy add-on |
| AdAway Default Blocklist | 6,540 | ours, because *this plan* started from the registry's filter_2. Oldest and smallest thing in the set |

So the honest read on our own list pair is: filter_1 is carrying the house, and
AdAway is a 2023 hosts-format extra that mostly overlaps it. If Phase 3 widens, it
should widen to HaGeZi Pro+ or OISD in place of AdAway, not alongside it —
two rules of thumb from the sampled configs: keep the total under roughly 300k
rules, and don't stack lists from the same vendor line.

*** 3.7 Follow-up: swap AdAway out for HaGeZi Pro+ or OISD

The one booked next step. Not urgent, and it should wait for a quiet day like
everything else here does.

*Why:* =filter_2.txt= is the weakest thing we run. It is *hosts* format, its own
header says *last modified 2023-11-21*, its 6,540 rules lean Android and overlap
filter_1 heavily, and because it is hosts-format, an entry it blocks answers
=127.0.0.1= rather than =0.0.0.0= (measured: =nym1-ib.adnxs-simple.com=) -- the
client is pointed at itself, which fails fast locally but is a different class of
answer from every other block on the box. HaGeZi is what the sampled configs
actually run: current, ABP-format, and tiered so you can pick the aggression.

*The change is one entry,* and it keeps id 2 so AGH updates the existing storage
entry instead of adopting a new one:

#+begin_src nix
  filters = [
    { enabled = true; id = 1; name = "AdGuard DNS filter";
      url = "https://adguardteam.github.io/HostlistsRegistry/assets/filter_1.txt"; }
    { enabled = true; id = 2; name = "HaGeZi Pro+";
      url = "https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/hagezi/dns-blocklists@latest/adblock/pro.plus.txt"; }

#+end_src

OISD Big (=https://big.oisd.nl=) is the alternative if Pro+ turns out too sharp --
it has the better reputation for false positives and costs about the same. Pick
*one*: HaGeZi and OISD together roughly double the rule count for the same
coverage, and stacking two tiers from one vendor line buys noise, not reach.

*Cost:* measured 58 MB for 192,408 rules, call it ~300 bytes per rule, so Pro+
(+~150k) is around +45 MB against 7 GB. Measure once after the swap, as in *3.1*.

*How to tell it went well:* =filtering/status= shows the new =rules_count= under a
minute of startup, =data/filters/2.txt= is replaced not added to, and then the day
rules -- both tables from a client, plus the query log for =REJECTED= rows against
anything a device here actually needs. The six AdAway-only names from *Phase 3 as
run* (=epom.com=, =tracker.marinsm.com=, and friends) are the interesting control:
if they come back answering *with addresses*, HaGeZi is narrower on those, which
is a coverage judgement to make knowingly rather than a bug.

*Rollback:* revert the entry. Both halves are one redeploy, and =systemctl stop
adguardhome= is the lever in between.

Decisions 1, 3 and 4 are one decision wearing three hats. DoH on AGH alone does

*Two corrections from the Phase-3 run.* First, that ordering assumed a leak; the
leak was measured and is not there. 12/12 unique cache-missed names from a client
reached AGH and its ~/etc/resolv.conf~ resolvers answered none of them, so
"dnsmasq is still leaking to the ISP" is only true when =127.0.0.1#5354= *refuses*

already half-made, by someone else: see the next section.

** Decision 1 has been made for you, by =94.140.14.14=

=94.140.14.14= is not a plain recursive resolver — it is AdGuard's own *filtering*
service. Measured 2026-10-17, before this file had any filters declared:

#+begin_src bash

dig +short google-analytics.com @94.140.14.14  # -> 0.0.0.0
dig +short google-analytics.com @1.1.1.1       # -> 142.250.73.68 (real)

So three things this plan said need restating:

- *The house was never unfiltered.* It has been ad-blocked server-side by AdGuard's
  DNS since decision 1 shipped, silently, with no declarative trace and no rule
  list anyone chose. Phase 3 is *less* mystery and *more* determinism than what it
  replaces, not more blocking.
- *=upstream_dns= currently pairs a blocking resolver with an unfiltered one under
  =load_balance= (confirmed in the =journalctl= startup line). For any name not in
  our own list, the answer depends on which one answers first. That is an
  accidental policy, not a design.
- *The DoH question should be asked against a non-blocking resolver.* Swapping to
  =https://dns.cloudflare.com/dns-query= (or quad9/nextdns, unfiltered) is the
  version of decision 1 that keeps the semantics and drops the plaintext; keeping
  an AdGuard DoH endpoint would just relocate the same undeclared server-side
  filtering into TLS.

For 4 the cheap half is: stop feeding dnsmasq the ISP's resolvers and give it
nothing but AGH (=no-resolv = true= in the same =settings= block that already
carries =bogus-priv= and =bind-interfaces=), then confirm from a client that a
name resolves only via AGH's upstreams. The risk of that switch is in *Known
limits*: it converts an AGH failure into an internet outage for the house.

Decision 2 is settled by the upstream module: the fork is obsolete, not just
stale.

- It exists because 2019-era AGH ran a setup wizard and kept its credential in
  =user.json=. Upstream's =preStart= now writes the generated yaml before the
  first start (=installFresh=), so *the wizard skip is not the fork's trick
  any more* — any =settings != null= skips it, which is what this plan does.
- Upstream deleted the =user.json= write. Recent AGH wants =auth.users= with a

- Upstream now sets =DynamicUser = true=, which removes the persistent

* Later additions, and what they change

Both of the things on the agenda next move one line, not the design — which is
the point of the loopback hop.

** unbound as AGH's upstream

~dns.upstream_dns = [ "127.0.0.1:5335" ]~ and nothing else. Chain order is
load-bearing: it must be =dnsmasq → AGH → unbound → root=. Anything that puts
unbound *ahead* of AGH deletes filtering silently.

Copy from =modules/adguard.nix= with care (deleted; it is in git history): unbound drops loopback-sourced
queries under ~do-not-query-localhost~ (its default), so that =127.0.0.1:5335=
pair may fall through rather than work. Verify it before trusting it.

** VPN, server-side (remote access into the LAN)

AGH does not move. dnsmasq grows a listener:

#+begin_src nix
  services.dnsmasq.settings.interface = [ "br-lan" "wg0" ];   # + listen-address
  networking.nat.internalInterfaces = [ "br-lan" "wg0" ];
  networking.firewall.interfaces."wg0".allowedUDPPorts = [ 53 67 ];
#+end_src

That last line is not optional: the =:53=/=67= opens are scoped to =br-lan=, so a
tunnel peer would get routing but no DNS. It is also why binding AGH =0.0.0.0=
would have been the wrong shortcut — per-interface DNS is dnsmasq's job, not
AGH's.

** VPN, client-side (the board's own traffic through a tunnel)

This is the one that edits the plan rather than appending to it:

- "upstream = public IPs, reachable via NAT" stops being true, and plain-UDP DNS
  either goes to the provider's resolver or leaks around the tunnel with no
  kill-switch. It makes decision 1 a DoH/DoT decision.
- ~networking.nat~ gains a second masquerade path and ~/etc/resolv.conf~ stops
  meaning what it means today (same reason =resolveLocalQueries = false= is fine
  now, and the same reason a =tailscaled --accept-dns= would rewrite it).
- On this board a tunnel is not just =services.*=: the Phase-5.6/5.8 kernel
  patches in =hosts/mono-gateway/default.nix= exist because policy-based IPsec
  SAs were being handed to cmm and breaking ESP, and =docs/mono-gateway-build-plan.org=
  measures tunnels at *software* speed because the FMan offload does not cover
  tunnel interfaces. WireGuard is the cheap path; strongSwan inherits the kernel fight.

** unbound + a VPN client, together

Mostly counterproductive: recursion through a tunnel is visible to that tunnel's
network anyway, costs root-server latency, and buys nothing a trusted DoH
upstream does not already buy. Pick one as "who I ask last".

Suggested order: AGH now → WG remote access → (only on a real reason) a VPN
client → unbound last, and only if the client stays off.

** Decision 2 as run — a login and one https URI

Wanted: log in to the UI, and reach it as an https URL rather than a tunnel. What
that took, in order of how much it cost to find out:

1. *The key is =users=, not =auth.users=.* This is the whole story. On 0.107.71
   the list is a *top-level* =users:= key — the =auth { realm, session_ttl,
   users }= block belongs to AGH 1.x. Declared the newer way it is parsed away in
   exactly the shape *Phase 3 as run* found for =filters=: =--check-config= says
   "configuration file is ok", the unit logs
   =no users in the configuration file; authentication is disabled=, the UI keeps
   answering =200= to anonymous =/control/status=, and the state file comes back
   holding AGH's own =users: []=. Nothing distinguishes a working declaration from
   a dropped one except that log line and the status code.

   The same lesson twice in two days, in the same config. The pattern is that
   0.107.71 is *silently tolerant*: it parses what it knows and drops the rest,
   so a wrong location or a wrong key name cannot fail the build and cannot fail
   the service — only the security property is lost, quietly. When a setting here
   does not visibly do something, the question to ask is "where does this binary
   read it", and the answer is on the box: what AGH *writes back* into
   =/var/lib/AdGuardHome/AdGuardHome.yaml= is the layout it reads.

2. *The login endpoint wants JSON.* =POST /control/login= with a form body returns
   =400 json decode=, which looks like a broken endpoint. The cookie is
   =agh_session= afterwards.

3. *The module's own pre-start check lies about this one thing.* It runs
   =--check-config= on the state file *before* merging, so every start logs
   =no users ... authentication is disabled= even when it is configured. Judge it
   by the unit process's log, not the pre-start's.

4. *Binding br-lan retires the tunnel form.* =host = "192.168.1.1"= means 3000 no
   longer answers on =127.0.0.1=, so the tunnel is
   =ssh -N -L 3000:192.168.1.1:3000 root@192.168.1.1=, not the =127.0.0.1= form
   this file used to show. Since 2026-10-18 =dns.bind_hosts= is =192.168.1.1= and
   =:53= is AGH's, so the check that matters is now =udp 192.168.1.1:53 =
   AdGuardHome=, with dnsmasq parked on =:5354= — out of the house's reach, and
   worth confirming from a client rather than from the board.

** What the query log can say about a client

*Since 2026-10-18 the client field carries house addresses,* because AGH took
=:53= and dnsmasq moved behind it — which is what that swap was *for*, and worth
stating first because everything below this line is the history of why it took a
reordering to get it. Until then every row said =127.0.0.1=, and the reason was
structural rather than a missing flag:

#+begin_src bash
# how it read until gen 31
ssh root@192.168.1.1 'curl -s "localhost:3000/control/querylog?limit=200&response_type=json" \
  | grep -o "\"client\":\"[^\"]*\"" | sort | uniq -c'
#      200 "client":"127.0.0.1"
#+end_src

dnsmasq forwarded from its own socket, so the source address that reached AGH was
the resolver's, and no address for a house machine was ever in the packet. AGH's
log answered *what the house asked* and could not answer *who asked* — and the
same fact moved the other way on the swap: dnsmasq's own log is what says
=127.0.0.1= now, since AGH is its only client.

The =auto_clients= list looks like it should fix that, and is a separate thing:

#+begin_src yaml
clients.runtime_sources: {arp: true, rdns: true, dhcp: true}   # whois too
dns.local_ptr_upstreams: []      # nothing to ask for PTR of 192.168.1.x
dhcp: {enabled: false}           # so no lease file is read
#+end_src

It is populated from ARP — ={"ip":"192.168.1.180","name":"","source":"ARP"}= —
which is a host inventory, not attribution. =rdns= has nowhere to ask and the
=dhcp= source has no path, even though the lease file is one filesystem away
(=/var/lib/dnsmasq/dnsmasq.leases=). So two edits are worth making for the
dashboard's own sake, and neither has been tested:

1. =dns.local_ptr_upstreams = [ "127.0.0.1:53" ]= — dnsmasq answers PTR for every
   lease it owns, which is the one thing here that knows the answer.
2. a lease path for the =dhcp= source — the same facts twice, in the block that
   also holds AGH's own (disabled) DHCP server, so read the keys first.

Neither of those puts a house IP into a query row, because that is a consequence
of the hop order and not a setting — which is the whole reason the order was
reversed the following day. This section is the record of the two steps that
proved it out:

1. *gen 30 (2026-10-17)* took the cheap half of the question instead of the
   topology: ~log-queries = true~ in ~services.dnsmasq.settings~, so
   =journalctl -u dnsmasq= carried the per-machine view
   (=query[A] example.com from 192.168.1.155=) while AGH's log stayed the record of
   what the house asked. It worked — six distinct askers in the first three
   minutes, ~17 KB of journal per 10 min — and the two things it had going against
   it were that the view lives in journald, not in the filter, and that 2.91 has no
   =log-queries-nomisc= or =freq= to trim PTR and mDNS chatter, so journald's 50M
   window is hours of history rather than days.
2. *gen 31 (2026-10-18)* reversed the order, and that key was deleted with the
   swap: once AGH stands upstream, dnsmasq's log says =127.0.0.1= for every row
   like AGH's used to. Attribution belongs in the filter now, which is where the
   UI shows it.

So the =auto_clients= discussion above is still about the wrong thing for
attribution — ARP is a host inventory, not attribution, and that did not change —
but =local_ptr_upstreams= gained a reason to exist: dnsmasq answers PTR for the
leases it owns, and it is now one hop away behind AGH rather than one hop in front.

** Shipping the log off the board

Not this plan, and as of 2026-10-18 *not for eMMC wear either* — see the
measurement below, which is what closed that concern. AGH has no push, so the
design is to pull =GET /control/querylog= from the log box on a timer
(=/control/querylog_search= 404s on 0.107.71); the rest of the reasoning and the
alternatives are the agenda item linked in *Cross-links*. What this file does own
are the facts that design has to survive: the retention interval below, that rows
carry house addresses only since gen 31 (*What the query log can say about a
client*), and now the write cost.

*** The eMMC-wear argument is measured, and it is not a reason (2026-10-18)

Someone asked whether the board should ship its query log off the eMMC to spare
the flash. It should not, and the numbers are the answer:

| Written per day | Source |
|-----------------+--------|
| ~26 MB | =data/querylog.json= — 72,856 entries over 25.5 h, 360 B/entry (so ~68k queries/day) |
| ~4.6 MB | the daily filter refresh (=1.txt= 4.4 MB + =2.txt= 0.2 MB, =filters_update_interval: 24=) |
| 279 MB total, all writers | =/proc/diskstats= mmcblk0 over the 29.5 h since boot |
| 4 KB | what a 60 s sample wrote — the log appends on the ring's 1000-entry wrap, not per query |

The board is =/dev/mmcblk0p1=, 32 GB ext4 mounted =rw,noatime,commit=60=, and
journald is volatile (=/var/log/journal= does not exist), so the mitigations that
matter for flash wear are already on. At the log's ~9.5 GB/year, and granting a
hostile write-amplification factor of 4 against a pessimistic 1000 P/E cycles on
32 GB (~8 TBW), the query log is worth ~0.12% of endurance a year. Shipping it
buys a daemon, credentials and a failure mode to save a rounding error.

What is worse: the only variant that removes the write is the lossy one.
=querylog.file_enabled = false= leaves the in-memory ring, and =size_memory=
1000 entries is ~20 minutes of traffic at this query rate — so a poller would run
constantly and still drop bursts, which destroys the one use the log exists for
(*3.4*: a day of real traffic, and per-client rows since gen 31). If history that
survives a re-flash is what is actually wanted, pull it — a timer on misfit
copying the day's =querylog.json=, or curling the ring — because a pull leaves the
board's write pattern exactly as it is, and that is the point.

* Known limits

- `shortcut: filter lists update on AGH's own daily timer over HTTPS, so a board
  without WAN egress serves an empty block cache; upgrade to a nix-provided cache
  dir (or a timer-fed fetch) if filtering must work with eth2 dark.`
- *The query log file rotates far more slowly than the shipping plan assumes.*
  Shipped is =querylog.interval: 2160h= — 90 days — with =size_memory: 1000=
  matching the ring. =data/querylog.json= is a file that almost never rotates:
  fine to poll, but declare =querylog.interval= if 24h is what the design wants
  (spelling matters: =querylog_interval= is the pre-schema-32 flat name, it is in
  the binary only on the migration path), and choose the retention rather than
  discovering it later. =interval= is this binary's only retention knob —
  =grep -a 'yaml:"retention"'= the 0.107.71 binary finds nothing, so there is no
  separate keep-window to set. 90 days at the measured 26 MB/day is ~2.3 GB
  against 27 GB free.
- *The query-log keys are top level too, and were in =dns:= since Phase 1.* Same
  trap, same mechanism, third time: at schema 32 the log's settings are a
  top-level =querylog:= map (=enabled=, =file_enabled=, =interval=,
  =size_memory=, =ignored=, =dir_path=), so =settings.dns.querylog_enabled=
  parses away silently and AGH runs the block off its own defaults. It sat that
  way from gen 22 to gen 33 unnoticed because the defaults happen to match what
  we declared — the tell is that the state yaml's =dns:= map never grew the keys,
  and the consequence was that *2.5*'s "turn the log off in a rebuild" mitigation
  could not have worked. Fixed 2026-10-18 (gen 33) by declaring
  ~settings.querylog = { enabled = true; file_enabled = true; }~ beside
  ~filters~. =checkPhase= accepts both spellings, so as with =filters= and
  =users=, the location is re-checked on every =adguardhome= bump by reading the
  keys AGH wrote into =/var/lib/AdGuardHome/AdGuardHome.yaml=, not by reading the
  generated yaml.
- *Per-client rows exist only since gen 31,* and the mirror image of the old
  problem is now the limit: AGH's log names the asker, and *dnsmasq's* log is the
  one that says =127.0.0.1= for every row, because AGH is its only client. So
  "the journal is quiet" says nothing about which machine was quiet — read AGH.
  See *What the query log can say about a client*.
- *Two services in series, either one able to take the house down.* A dead
  dnsmasq is a full outage now, =.lan= and =*.yuanw.me= included, not just external
  resolution. The mitigation shipped with the swap is
  ~systemd.services.dnsmasq.serviceConfig={RestartSec,StartLimitIntervalSec}~ so a
  transient =address already in use= across a restart heals itself; the lever back
  is still ~just mono-gateway-rollback~.
- *AGH's =:53= and dnsmasq's =5354= both live on =br-lan's* address; only the
  firewall keeps the old resolver out of the house's reach (nothing opens 5354,
  probed from a client). It is a weaker guarantee than the loopback bind this
  replaced, which is why the accept-rule sentence is in the config comment too.
- *The filter list is =settings.filters=, not =settings.dns.filters=.* This is the
  most expensive fact in the file. At schema 32 the key is a sibling of =dns=, and
  the same list nested inside =settings.dns= raises no error, no log line and no
  blocklist: AGH parses it, never sees it, and writes back the pair it compiles
  in. Both spellings evaluate, =--check-config= likes both, and
  =/control/filtering/status= looks plausible either way, so the only tell is the
  rule count. Note that the ecosystem is *not* the cautionary tale here — all 15
  public configs found by code search declare it as a sibling of =dns=, correctly.
  Ours was nested, and got no signal for it. Re-check the location on every
  =adguardhome= bump; =grep -a 'yaml:"filters"'= the binary is the cheap way to
  find where it reads.
- *Port 3000 is open to the whole LAN, and the direct path is plain http.* The
  rule is scoped to =br-lan= (so not eth2) but not to misfit, because
  source-scoping needs an nft rule of our own and there is no precedent for one in
  this repo. Anything on the LAN can reach the login page over http without going
  through =https://agh.yuanw.me= — the bcrypt hash is what guards that, and
  sniffing the login is the residual risk. Tightening =192.168.1.190= alone is a
  small nft table, and it is the right change if a device on the LAN ever matters
  enough to worry about.
- *The =users= key name is binary-specific.* =auth.users= parses away on 0.107.71
  and works on 1.x; see *Decision 2 as run*. Re-check after any
  =adguardhome= bump, the same way =filters= location gets re-checked.
- *Every =/control= check needs a session now.* The runbooks in this file were
  written against an open UI; anonymous calls are =401=, and the login is JSON.
- No =blocking_mode= is declared, so =blocking_mode: default= applies — and
  *measured, that answers a blocked name with =0.0.0.0=*, not NXDOMAIN; the
  hosts-format AdAway entries come back as =127.0.0.1= for the same reason. A
  client told "here, 0.0.0.0" fails a little more quietly than one told "no such
  domain", which is good or bad depending on what you want the log to show.
  =refused=/=null_ip=/=custom_ip= exist, but the exact spelling is
  version-dependent — and the module's =checkPhase= runs
  =AdGuardHome -c … --check-config=, so a wrong value fails the *build*, not the
  router. That is also the fastest way to learn what this AGH accepts.
- ~services.adguardhome~'s *module* is 25.11's too, not ours, and whether it maps
  =host=/=port= onto =http.address= or the old =bind_host= keys depends on the
  schema version that release carries. The Phase-1 build answered that: at
  =schema_version: 32= the yaml carries =http.address: 127.0.0.1:3000=. What is
  still only provable on the box is that the bind actually took:
  ~ss -ltnp | grep 3000~ after the deploy — =0.0.0.0:3000= there means it did not,
  and the firewall is the only thing left.
- The AP's AGH is installed and inert for DNS, but not free: if its init service
  is enabled it still fetches list updates and writes to the flash. Once the
  board's AGH is working, remove the package and its service there — an
  =openwrt/routers/e8450-ap.nix= decision, same class as leaving its dnsmasq
  running.
- Declaring =filters = [ ]= while adding lists in the UI is a slow wipe: the
  declarative key wins the merge and replaces the list on the next rebuild
  (=yaml-merge= replaces lists, verified). Fix and options in *2.6*, and the
  declarative form in *3.1*.
- *Rule-list membership is a fact about the day it was measured.* The 185,488
  rules and the four blocked domains in 3.3 are from a download on 2026-10-17.
  Lists are re-fetched daily; do not treat that table as a permanent oracle, and
  re-run both directions of it after any long gap rather than trusting the
  numbers.
- *Allowlist rules live in the yaml, not in a file.* =user_rules= (and
  =whitelist_filters=) are top-level keys alongside =filters=, so what the UI adds
  survives every rebuild -- =settings= declares neither, and =yaml-merge= only
  touches keys the declaration declares. The corollary is the one to remember:
  the moment you declare =user_rules=, the declaration *replaces* whatever is in
  the box, so declare a permanent rule instead of pasting one in, and expect the
  UI's copy to disappear. Details and the nix shape in *3.4* lever (2).
- *The generated yaml does not prove the DNS bind.* =dns.port= is the key
  0.107.71 reads; =bind_port= is accepted, ignored, and leaves =:53=. That is the
  one Phase-1 blind spot the run had to catch (gen 21 crash-looped), so after any
  change here the check is ~ss -lunp | grep :5354~ on the board, not the yaml.
- *AGH is a gate now, not advice.* =no-resolv= went in with *decision 4*, so
  dnsmasq holds one upstream (=127.0.0.1#5354=) and 5354's refusal is the end of
  the line: external names fail, local names survive. What that buys is the
  guarantee that nothing skips the filter; what it costs is that an AGH outage is
  now a DNS outage, which is why ~just mono-gateway-rollback~ is the lever and not
  ~systemctl stop adguardhome~ (*2.4*). Any decision about DoH (1) or a redirect
  (3) inherits this: there is no longer a path inside dnsmasq that bypasses AGH.
- ~blocking_mode = "refused"~ would be quieter than NXDOMAIN but tells a probing
  client *that* it was blocked rather than *that it does not exist*. Not declared
  here, so NXDOMAIN applies; flip it deliberately if that ever matters.
